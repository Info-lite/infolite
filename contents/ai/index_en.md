What is AI?
-----------

AI (Artificial Intelligence) is a technology that makes computers mimic human intelligence. **Generative AI**, which has been developing rapidly in recent years, can generate text, images, videos, audio, etc., just like a human.

In this chapter, we learn how AI, including generative AI, is utilized in society, as well as the precautions for its utilization.


The History of AI and Machine Learning
-------------

In the 1950s, a mathematician Alan Turing proposed the concept of AI with the question, "Can machines think?" Subsequently, the term "AI" was coined at the Dartmouth Conference held in 1956.

#### Rule-Based AI
Early AI attempted to make AI behave like humans by writing out human thought processes step-by-step as programs. Based on this approach, computer systems with knowledge of specific fields, called "expert systems," emerged. Programs such as Mycin (1970s, medicine) and Cyc (1980s, encyclopedia) made AI widely recognized by the public.

However, there were significant challenges for real-world application, such as the need for humans to write down all rules in advance, and the inability to apply it to fields with many exceptions.

#### Machine Learning
Entering the 2000s, along with the development of the internet, collecting vast amounts of data (**big data**) became easy, and computer processing speeds also improved dramatically. Against this background, instead of humans teaching rules, methods for **automatically learning rules from given data (machine learning)** attracted attention. Machine learning improved AI accuracy, drawing attention back to AI, particularly in the fields of image recognition and text processing.

#### Deep Learning
In recent years,**neural networks** modelled after the human brain have made it possible for computers to perform deeper processing. With the advent of neural networks, modern AI utilizing massive amounts of data and computational resources emerged rapidly. The **Transformer** model, published by Google researchers in 2017, dramatically improved the accuracy of AI handling natural language and serves as the foundation for many generative AIs.

How Generative AI Works
-----------

Let's look at how AI handling natural language, such as ChatGPT (OpenAI) and Gemini (Google), works.

These **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** AIs can create natural text as if having a conversation with a human. In the system, a program called a **Large Language Model (LLM)** runs. An LLM generates text by **predicting the continuation of a given sentence** based on text from the internet, books, and other sources.

For example, suppose there is the sentence: "**Tokyo Metropolitan University is located in ____.**" What kind of words could fit in the blank? The LLM returns the most likely word based on context from candidates such as "Japan," "Tokyo," or "Hachioji." Furthermore, if the sentence changes to "Tokyo Metropolitan University is a ____.", the candidates will also change to "public university," "university," "university with plenty of greenery," etc.

Modern Society and AI
-----------

Today, AI is utilized in various situations, and many technologies have been put into practical use. In 2024, AI-related fields made headlines by winning both the Nobel Prize in Physics and the Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Technologies already in practical use include the following:

#### AI Utilization in Daily Life

Many AI technologies are already used in various services we use in our daily lives.

Translation: DeepL, Google Translate, Mirai Translate

Smartphones: Voice assistants (Siri, Google Assistant, Alexa)

E-commerce sites: Product recommendations, personalized advertising, recommendations on social media and video streaming sites

Weather information: Weather forecasting and prediction of extreme weather


#### AI Utilization in Medicine

Diagnostic Support: Technology development is underway to detect lesions in X-ray and MRI images and suggest them to physicians.

Drug Discovery: One of the research achievements that won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2024 was the computational prediction of protein 3D structures. Using this technology, research is actively underway to develop new drugs targeted at proteins that cause diseases.


#### AI Utilization in Industry

Customer Support: Chatbot AIs capable of responding 24 hours a day, 365 days a year

Business: Prediction of customer traffic and demand
Manufacturing: AI for quality control and defect detection in factories

Transportation & Logistics: Autonomous driving technology for cars and buses, traffic congestion prediction, and navigation systems



AI and Ethics
----

#### AI Literacy
AI literacy refers to the ability to understand how AI works, recognize its limitations, and utilize it appropriately. In modern society, individuals are required not only to use AI tools but also to properly evaluate the reliability and bias of AI-generated outputs, thereby avoiding misinformation and inappropriate usage. For example, in university life, instead of using AI tools to write reports and assignments on one's behalf, it is necessary to limit their use to supporting tasks such as brainstorming or creating outlines, recognizing that the final responsibility lies with oneself.

#### Consideration for Copyright, Plagiarism, and Piracy
The generation of text, images, and videos by AI tools often uses existing copyrighted works as training data, and the outputs may unintentionally infringe upon the copyrights of others. Therefore, when utilizing outputs from AI tools, extreme caution must be taken to avoid copyright infringement and plagiarism. Specifically, rather than directly copying and pasting AI outputs, it is important to express the ideas in your own words.

#### Personal Information
Currently, many AI tools operate on the cloud via the internet, and input information is sent to and stored on servers provided by the service operators. Due to security concerns, on-device AIs, which perform all processing within one's own device, are also gradually becoming popular. When using AI tools, it is crucial to understand how input information is handled and to exercise sufficient caution in handling personal and confidential information.

#### Hallucination
AI tools sometimes generate information that sounds plausible but is factually incorrect. Specifically, they may generate incorrect figures or details, or present non-existent academic papers or websites as reference sources. Because this is caused by various factors, such as bias in the data used to train the AI tools or ambiguity in prompts, it is impossible for users to completely prevent hallucinations. Rather than blindly trusting the outputs of AI tools, users must verify the information by referencing reliable sources.
